A simple yet essential task! Boiling water is a fundamental process that involves heating water to its boiling point, which is 212°F (100°C) at sea level. Here's a step-by-step guide on how to boil water:

**Equipment needed:**

* A pot with a lid (preferably stainless steel or heat-resistant glass)
* Water
* A stove or electric kettle
* Optional: a thermometer (if you want to check the temperature)

**Instructions:**

1. **Fill the pot with water**: Pour cold water into the pot until it's about 3/4 full.
2. **Place the pot on the stove or electric kettle**: Position the pot on a medium-sized flame or heat setting, depending on your stove or electric kettle.
3. **Turn on the heat**: If using a stove, turn the knob to the "on" position. If using an electric kettle, plug it in and select the desired temperature or mode (e.g., boiling).
4. **Wait for the water to boil**: As the water heats up, you'll start to see bubbles forming at the bottom of the pot. When the bubbles rise to the surface and start popping, the water has reached a rolling boil.
5. **Check the temperature (optional)**: If you have a thermometer, insert it into the water to ensure it's reached 212°F (100°C).
6. **Turn off the heat**: Once the water is boiling, turn off the stove or electric kettle.
7. **Let it cool slightly (optional)**: If you're planning to use the boiled water for cooking, let it cool down a bit before proceeding.

**Tips and precautions:**

* Always use cold water when filling the pot to prevent scalding.
* Make sure the pot is secure on the stove or electric kettle to prevent it from sliding or falling off.
* Be careful when removing the lid as steam will be released, and the water may still be hot.
* Never leave boiling water unattended, as it can suddenly boil over and cause accidents.

That's it! Boiling water is a simple process that requires attention to temperature and safety precautions.

**Step 5: Let It Boil!**

Once your water has reached a boil, let it keep boiling for at least 1-2 minutes. This ensures any bacteria or impurities in the water are killed. After that, you can turn off the heat and carefully remove the pot from the stove.
