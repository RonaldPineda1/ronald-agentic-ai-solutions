Boiling water is a straightforward process that typically requires a pot, water, and a heat source, such as a stovetop. At sea level, water boils at 100 
∘ C (212 ∘ F), which is the point where it rapidly converts to steam.

Here are the simple steps to boil water on a stove:

Select a Pot: Choose a pot large enough to hold the desired amount of water, leaving a few inches of space from the rim to prevent boiling water from splashing out. A disc-base pot with thin stainless steel sidewalls can be very efficient for boiling water quickly.

Add Water: Pour fresh, cold tap water into the pot.

Apply Heat: Place the pot on the burner and turn the heat to the highest setting.

Cover (Optional but Recommended): Place a lid snugly on the pot. Covering the pot traps heat and steam, which significantly speeds up the boiling process.

Wait for a Rolling Boil: Wait for the water to reach a full rolling boil. This is when large, active bubbles are continuously and rapidly breaking the surface across the entire pot.

Use as Needed: Once the water is boiling, turn off the heat and carefully use the water for cooking, cleaning, or other purposes.

The Stages of Boiling
As water heats up, it progresses through several visual stages before reaching a full rolling boil:

Slow Simmer: Very tiny bubbles form on the bottom of the pot, and you'll see steam beginning to rise from the water's surface.

Simmer: The heat increases, and there's a gentle bubbling with small to medium-sized bubbles rising and breaking the surface.

Rapid Simmer: The heat is higher, and bubbling becomes more aggressive, but the bubbles are still relatively small.

Rolling Boil: This is the highest stage of boiling, characterized by many large, vigorous bubbles rolling over the entire surface of the pot.

The first step to any great recipe is sometimes the simplest: boiling water! While it seems basic, knowing the steps will make you a more confident cook.

Here is a simple explanation of how to boil water:

1. Gather Your Supplies 💧
You'll need a few items:

Pot: Choose a pot that is appropriately sized for the amount of water you need. A medium saucepan works well for most needs.

Water: Use cold tap water. Cold water is generally recommended because hot water can sometimes contain more dissolved minerals or impurities from your pipes.

Stove or Hot Plate: This is your heat source.

2. Add Water to the Pot 🏺
Pour the cold water into the pot.

Do not fill the pot to the very top. Leave at least an inch or two of space between the water and the rim. This prevents the water from boiling over when it gets hot.

3. Place the Pot on the Heat Source 🔥
Put the pot on a burner on your stove.

Turn the burner setting to High or Medium-High. Using high heat will bring the water to a boil fastest.

4. Observe the Stages of Boiling 👀
As the water heats up, you will see three main stages:

Simmering (Shimmering or Steaming): Small bubbles will start to form on the bottom of the pot, and you may see steam rising from the surface. The water is hot, but not yet boiling.

Rolling Boil (The Goal!): The water is boiling rapidly and vigorously. Large bubbles will rise quickly and continuously break the surface, and the bubbling won't stop even if you stir it. This is the point you are usually aiming for when a recipe calls for "boiling water," such as when cooking pasta.

Vigorous Boil (Rapid Boil): This is an even more intense, violent bubbling.

5. Use Caution and Turn Off the Heat 🌡️
Once the water reaches a rolling boil, turn the heat down to whatever the recipe requires (often a simmer or medium-low to maintain the boil).

When you are done, turn the burner completely off.

Be careful! Steam is very hot and can cause burns, so keep your hands away from the rising steam. The pot handle and sides will also be hot. Use an oven mitt or towel if you need to handle the pot.

You've successfully boiled water!

Boiling water is a fundamental cooking technique. Here are the steps to boil water, from selecting a pot to reaching the boiling point.

Steps for Boiling Water
Choose a Pot: Select a pot or saucepan with a volume greater than the amount of water you plan to boil. A pot with a snug-fitting lid is ideal, as the lid helps trap heat and speeds up the boiling process.

Add Water: Pour fresh, cold tap water into the pot. Leave at least a few inches of space between the water surface and the rim of the pot to prevent overflow when the water starts boiling vigorously.

Apply Heat: Place the pot on the stove burner and set the heat to high. Cover the pot with the lid.

Wait and Observe: As the water heats up, you'll observe stages of bubbling:

Simmer: Small bubbles appear around the bottom and sides of the pot, and steam begins to rise.

Rapid Simmer/Lazy Boil: More bubbles form and gently break the surface, often in slow, distinct streams.

Reaching the Boiling Point: Wait until the water reaches a rolling boil, which is characterized by large, continuous bubbles rapidly breaking across the entire surface of the water, and the bubbling cannot be stopped by stirring.

The Boiling Point
The boiling point is the temperature at which the vapor pressure of the liquid equals the pressure exerted on the liquid by the surrounding atmosphere.

At sea level, the boiling point of pure water is 212°F (100°C).

The boiling point decreases at higher altitudes because the atmospheric pressure is lower.
