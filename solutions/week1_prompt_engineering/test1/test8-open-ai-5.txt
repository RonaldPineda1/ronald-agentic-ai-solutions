Here’s a clear, step-by-step explanation of how to boil water:

🧂 Step 1: Choose the Right Pot

Pick a pot or kettle large enough for the amount of water you need. Make sure it’s clean and suitable for your heat source (stovetop, electric kettle, etc.).

💧 Step 2: Add Water

Fill the pot with cold or room-temperature water. Don’t fill it all the way to the top—leave some space to prevent boiling over.

🔥 Step 3: Place on Heat

Put the pot on the stove and turn the burner to high heat. If you’re using an electric kettle, close the lid and turn it on.

👀 Step 4: Wait and Watch

After a few minutes, you’ll see small bubbles forming at the bottom.
Then:

Simmering: Small, steady bubbles—around 85–95°C (185–203°F).

Boiling: Large, rolling bubbles and lots of steam—100°C (212°F) at sea level.

🫗 Step 5: Turn Off the Heat

Once it’s boiling, turn off the burner (or let the kettle click off). Use the water immediately, or let it cool depending on your purpose.

⚠️ Safety Tip

Always handle hot pots or kettles carefully—use oven mitts, and avoid leaning directly over steam.

Here’s a clear, beginner-friendly, step-by-step guide to boiling water — starting with choosing a pot and ending when it reaches its boiling point:

🥣 Step 1 — Choose the Right Pot

Select a pot that:

Is clean and made of metal (stainless steel or aluminum).

Has enough space so the water won’t spill when it boils.

Has a lid, which helps it boil faster.

➡️ Example:
For 2–3 cups of water, a small saucepan is perfect.
For pasta or soup, use a large pot.

🚰 Step 2 — Add Water

Place the pot in the sink.

Fill it with cold tap water up to the amount you need.

Leave some space at the top (about 1 inch or 2 cm) to prevent overflow.

💡 Tip: Cold water usually tastes fresher than warm tap water because it contains less dissolved metal from pipes.

🔥 Step 3 — Place the Pot on the Stove

Put the pot on a burner that matches its size.

Make sure the pot sits flat and stable.

🔥 Step 4 — Turn On the Heat

Turn the burner to high heat.

If your stove has numbers, use the highest setting.

Optional: Cover the pot with its lid — it traps heat and makes the water boil faster.

👀 Step 5 — Wait and Watch

As the water heats up:

Tiny bubbles form on the bottom (around 160 °F / 70 °C).

The bubbles rise slowly and small wisps of steam appear (around 180 °F / 80 °C).

Finally, it reaches a rolling boil — large bubbles constantly burst at the surface and steam rises continuously (212 °F / 100 °C at sea level).

⚠️ Step 6 — Know When It’s Boiling

You’ve reached the boiling point when:

The surface is in continuous motion with big bubbles.

Steam is rising steadily.

If you stir it, the boiling doesn’t stop.

✅ Step 7 — Use It or Turn Off the Heat

For tea, coffee, or instant food, use the boiling water immediately.

For pasta or vegetables, keep it boiling while you cook.

When finished, turn off the burner and remove the pot carefully (use oven mitts!).
